# What a machine keeps, and the copies it holds

What the stack keeps on its machine, where each thing is and why, the copies of
itself the machine holds, taking a copy and putting one back, putting back one
run of changes the record shows, and putting its configuration back to
lemonfiber's own. The code is the `N6` half of `app-modules/kernel` and
`app-modules/sdk`, drawn by `WhatThisMachineKeepsHere`, `TakingACopyHere`,
`PuttingACopyBack`, `PuttingThatRunBack` and `PuttingTheConfigurationBack`.

Each row says what the requirement asks and what in this repository answers it.
The spec is canonical; where this page and a requirement disagree, the
requirement is right and this page is a defect.

| Requirement | What it asks | What keeps it |
|---|---|---|
| `N6-R1` | A rehearsed run is labelled as a rehearsal wherever its result is shown, and never described in the past tense | Putting a copy back always rehearses first: `PuttingACopyBack` heads the listing *a rehearsal: nothing has been put back*, and every sentence under it says what *would* happen. The listing and the report of a restore are drawn from different values, `WhatPuttingItBackWouldShow` and `HowPuttingItBackWent`, so one cannot be drawn as the other. A copy's report carries `WhetherItWasRehearsed`, and `HowACopyReads` words a rehearsed one in the conditional in every sentence — what it would copy, remove, come to and hold — under its own heading. `TakingACopyTest` and `PuttingACopyBackTest` assert both. Putting the configuration back is previewed rather than rehearsed: no action argument asks for a rehearsal, and `reset` without the yes is a preview. `PuttingTheConfigurationBack` heads it *a preview: nothing has been put back*, and `HowAResetReads` takes the tense from the stack's `confirmed` on `TheReset` rather than from whether the yes was sent, so a report the stack did not carry out is worded in the conditional even after a yes. `PuttingTheConfigurationBackTest` asserts both. Stopping seeding carries it too, and [how full a machine is](how-full-a-machine-is.md) says where. Putting a run back reads the stack's `rehearsed` into `WhetherItWasRehearsed` on `ARunPutBack`; `HowARunBackReads` picks every sentence of the report in the conditional for a rehearsal, and `PuttingThatRunBack` heads it *a rehearsal: nothing has been put back*. `PuttingARunBackTest` asserts it |
| `N6-R2` | The scope of a copy or a restore is stated before it starts and again on its result: the whole stack, one service, or an existing project | `ScopeOfACopy` has the three arms and no default, and `HowAScopeReads` is the one place each is worded. Taking a copy asks first, naming the scope `ACopyAsked` would send; the copy while it runs and its report both name it again. A restore's listing names the scope before the yes, and its report after. An existing setup is named with its project and the host paths it read |
| `N6-R3` | Copies removed by taking a new one are reported with that copy | `ACopyTaken` cannot be built without `TheCopies` it pruned, and `TheCopyTaken` refuses a report whose list is missing or holds something that is not a name. The report says how many it removed and names each; one that removed none says every other copy is still kept |
| `N6-R4` | A running copy shows how far it has got, and a paced one is told apart from a stalled one | Half kept. The contract carries how far a copy got only once it has finished: the `job` envelope a running copy answers with holds its action and its name and nothing else, so while it runs the screen says it is being taken, that the stack says nothing of its progress until it finishes. It asks on `HowOftenAScreenLooks`'s declared cadence while the copy runs. The finished report's `pace` is drawn as the stack gave it: `HowACopyPaced` carries the bytes, the budget and the stack's own verdict, and a copy past the budget is said to be slow because of what is kept rather than a fault. What a running copy has got through waits on the event stream or the contract |
| `N6-R5` | Where a restore put data somewhere other than where it came from, the app says so | `WhereTheDataGoes`, whose two arms are back where it was and elsewhere with both roots. The listing says where the data would go before the yes; the report says where it went. The yes re-points the data only where the listing said it would move, so what is agreed is what was shown |
| `N6-R6` | An undo names what it could not reverse and why, and a partial reversal is never presented as a complete one | `ARunPutBack` carries what went back, what was `left` with why, and what going back also means, and `leftNothing()` is the one place a reversal is called complete — only where nothing was left. `TheRunPutBack` refuses a report without `left`, or with a row of it missing its target or its reason, rather than reading it as one that left nothing. `PuttingThatRunBack` leads the report with what was left, each with the stack's reason, before what went back. The agreement before it comes from the record's own rows for the run — every change under its stamp and the count the record gives — because the stack takes no yes and offers no rehearsal of this over the wire; `ARunAgreedTo` is built only from those rows, and a run where a row says it cannot go back offers no yes, because the stack judges every change before touching any. A run the stack stopped on a problem — no run under the stamp, more than one, a change that cannot be reversed, nowhere to look, or a change it could not take back part of the way through — is `HowPuttingARunBackIsGoing::refused()`, carrying the stack's sentence, what it means and what it named as `ARefusalInItsWords`, and is drawn in those words with the road back to the record and never as a report. `PuttingARunBackTest` and `PuttingARunBackContractTest` assert it |
| `N6-R8` | Resetting configuration is described by what changed and which connections went with it, rather than by a reassurance | `PuttingTheConfigurationBack`, reached from the settings. The preview draws every file whose edits would go, each line of its diff marked as the stack marks it — the operator's `-`, lemonfiber's `+` — and every connection that would go back with them; a file that differs in no line that can be shown says so. A preview reverting nothing says nothing would change and offers no yes. The yes is `AResetAgreed`, which only a preview that would revert something produces, and the report after it names what went back and which connections went with it. `AStackEdit` refuses a diff line marked as neither side, and `WhatAResetReverts` refuses a report with a part missing, so a shorter preview is never drawn as the whole of one. A refusal follows `WhatARefusalMeant::inItsWords()` and is drawn through `RefusedInItsWords`: the stack's sentence, what it means and what it named. No sentence on the screen says the change is safe or harmless |
| `N1-R10` | A run the stack would not put back, a copy it will not restore and a stack that could not be reached are different things with different remedies | One rule, `WhatARefusalMeant::inItsWords()`, carries a problem document the stack sent at a status other than `401` or `403`, with a sentence in it, as `ARefusalInItsWords` — the sentence, what it means and what it named — and everything else, a sentence with no problem around it, a problem with no sentence or a body it cannot read, as the obstacle it is. `Reversers` hands it the refusal of a run's job. The listing of a copy is answered at once, so a copy the stack will not restore is refused there, and a yes to one is taken on as a job that can stop on a problem; `Restorers` hands it both. `PuttingThatRunBack` and `PuttingACopyBack` draw a refusal in the stack's words through `RefusedInItsWords`, offering the record for a run, the copies for a refused listing and reading the listing again for a refused yes, and offer asking again only for the obstacle, because the same question is answered the same way. `PuttingARunBackContractTest` and `PuttingBackContractTest` cover each refusal the stack documents for `undo` and `restore`, and `PuttingARunBackTest` and `PuttingACopyBackTest` what is drawn |
| `N6-R7` | What the stack is holding is shown with why, and nothing the contract marks secret has its value rendered | `SomethingKept`, which cannot be built without what it is, where, why and `WhetherItHoldsASecret`. The screen says on every row whether it holds a secret. No type between the wire and the glass has a field for a value: `WhatIsStored` reads the four fields it names and nothing else, so a value the stack sent beside a secret is dropped at the reader. `SeeingWhatThisMachineKeepsTest` sends one through the real adapter and asserts it is not drawn (`SomethingKeptTest`, `WhatThisMachineKeepsTest`, `WhatWasFoundKeptTest`, `WhetherItHoldsASecretTest`, `WhatIsStoredTest`, `StoringContractTest`) |
| `N6-R9` | An empty list of archives is told apart from one that could not be read | `WhatCopiesWereFound`, whose two arms are `TheCopies` and an obstacle. An empty `TheCopies` is drawn as *no copy has been taken*; an obstacle is drawn as its own sentence with what stood in the way and the remedy. `TheArchives` refuses a payload with no list, and a copy with no name, rather than reading either as a shorter list (`TheCopiesTest`, `WhatCopiesWereFoundTest`, `TheArchivesTest`, `CopyingContractTest`, `SeeingWhatThisMachineKeepsTest`) |
| `N6-R10` | Where the stack offers neither a rollback nor a restore, the app offers neither, and never presents one as the other | Putting back is offered on each copy the stack listed and on nothing else: an empty list and one that could not be read offer none. `PuttingACopyBack` offers the yes only where the stack listed the copy; a stack that would not list it, whether it refused in its own words or could not be reached, leaves nothing to agree to. The yes is `WhatPuttingItBackWouldDo`, which only a listing produces. The screen calls it putting a copy back throughout, and the update screen's rollback is not offered here |
| `N6-R11` | Nothing here offers first-run setup | The screens offer taking a copy, putting one back and asking again, and nothing else, which `SeeingWhatThisMachineKeepsTest` asserts on what it draws |

What the stack keeps is asked first, and an obstacle there is the screen's
obstacle. The copies are asked only once that has answered, so a stack that
could not be listed still has what it keeps shown.

What is on the machine and is not the stack's is listed beside what it keeps.
Whether the call that answered removed anything is recorded as not read in
`WhatThisAppDoesNotRead`, with its reason, and so is each
field of a copy and a restore that no screen draws.

## Asked for, and not drawn yet

The `Partial` state is an undo or a restore that finished with things it could
not do. Putting a run back draws it from `left`. The `restore` envelope carries
no part left undone — a restore either answers with what it did or is refused —
so nothing here draws a restore as partial. A restore refused part of the way
through is drawn in the stack's own words, which say where it stopped.
